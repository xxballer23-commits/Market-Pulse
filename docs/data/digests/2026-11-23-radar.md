Thin week for net-new sourcing. The strongest names are Rove (building its own loyalty stack), Revolut (airline currency links for fintech wallets), Etihad (new US co-brand), plus a few experience and dining plays. Most Bilt and Chase stories are competitor moves to benchmark, not partner leads.

- **Rove**: Travel rewards platform with its own points currency, travel portal, dining program and a growing list of transfer partners (launched about 18 months ago). ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/rove-elite-status/))
  - Signal: Launched an elite status program with up to every third hotel night free, plus shopping and transfer bonuses.
  - Why it fits: It is assembling a full loyalty stack quickly, which makes it a candidate for hotel perks, transfer access or offers aimed at affluent travelers.
  - Angle: Hotel night or portal benefit for premium cardholders, or a transfer-partner link into its currency.

- **Revolut x Velocity Points**: Fintech account provider that now gives customers access to the Velocity airline loyalty currency. ([Executive Traveller](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiYEFVX3lxTE4zQWpQZTBhVGZtRnlMcndGZUFjX0ZPbGFlZWZCMG11NjluVzNvQ2ZLcXBycy1GWUp5MjZ1S2w2OUVDY19mSkhvUjlTZ2QyeERNRHVYSnJidjlaNkdFdXgtaA?oc=5))
  - Signal: Revolut customers can now access Velocity Points, linking the fintech to an airline loyalty program.
  - Why it fits: Shows non-bank wallets are getting direct airline loyalty links, which is a competitive route to affluent travelers and a model for transfer-style deals.
  - Angle: Track how the Revolut link is structured and consider whether a similar airline loyalty tie-in could sit alongside our rewards currency.

- **Etihad**: Gulf airline with a premium long-haul product and loyalty program. ([Parade Magazine](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMi1AFBVV95cUxQaXl0UHl5SnEzTm8zU0MyVGlGVlMtbWxET1dYUVAyMEVvclZuMG5SWnBBTzFVOHc5VVFJQUJVNVY1NFh0WmpnTXhHMGRuTmNObk9wbjU3MHZEbWtQZXY5bWZOY0xNM2hPUlF0enYwZU1nQnAtZ3ZRU1pvbDFZQ0NsalkyM050SWxoWnVoVE1BMWczTG9HXzZiT3Y3OTl3ZkpPWFBFUlUzMzZuMGlfN2hfRlpZbjVueDNiQWhfWlhaRGg5WGFmem1pTTkyVzN5NXQwdWxKRg?oc=5))
  - Signal: Launched its first US credit cards, one with instant airline status and airport lounge access. Issuer not stated in the item.
  - Why it fits: A premium international carrier entering the US card market signals appetite for US issuer partnerships and a status-plus-lounge value proposition.
  - Angle: Etihad status or lounge benefit for a premium card, or a Gulf and Middle East travel perk. Confirm the issuer and exclusivity first.

- **Radisson**: Global hotel group launching its first paid membership. ([Skift](https://skift.com/2026/11/23/radissons-first-paid-membership-targets-diners-not-guests-exclusive/))
  - Signal: The paid membership targets local diners rather than hotel guests, with generous discounts.
  - Why it fits: A hotel brand building a local-spend product opens card-linked dining and local-experience angles, not just travel.
  - Angle: Complimentary or discounted membership as a cardholder dining and hotel benefit, tied to everyday local spend.

- **Ikon Pass x Atmos Unlocked**: Ski pass product now redeemable with Alaska Atmos points (65K for Ikon Base, 100K for the full pass). ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/buy-ikon-pass-with-alaska-atmos-points-65k-base-100k-full-ikon-pass/))
  - Signal: Alaska's Atmos Unlocked lets members spend points on Ikon ski passes.
  - Why it fits: Points-for-experience redemption in a high-income lifestyle category (skiing) is a template for non-travel redemption options.
  - Angle: Ski or outdoor pass as a redemption or statement-credit style benefit for affluent cardholders.

- **Disney x Visa (Hong Kong Disneyland)**: Network-level entertainment partnership between Disney and Visa. ([Pirates and Princesses](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMimgFBVV95cUxOOEhBSEFFSDdvdDlVTTJHSk9HNUFIRVN3ZFlBM01lZ2VtOXlrNGxUNUVTZ2RVM2taT2ZmMFMyeUlPY3ZUd1Nnazl0SFc4MGZhdEZKVFdhUExHVlhxRmtyV2JqX1RPZ0YtTlVGR1hJOEliNjNYdllxRnBjSkNfWjRodnVwQklObkZ1TDcxNWZkclZDWEk5bkwyTk13?oc=5))
  - Signal: Visa cardholders get early entry to Hong Kong Disneyland under the new deal.
  - Why it fits: Early-entry access is a low-cost, high-perceived-value perk that appeals to affluent families, and it is a competitor-network play worth countering.
  - Angle: Theme-park early entry or priority access benefit negotiated at the issuer level in US destinations. Confirm which networks and cards are covered.

- **Resy Rewards**: Restaurant reservation platform whose rewards program pays an extra 4X Membership Rewards points on dining. ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/resy-rewards-earn-an-additional-4x-membership-rewards-points-on-dining/))
  - Signal: Resy Rewards now adds 4X Membership Rewards points on dining for eligible cardholders.
  - Why it fits: It is an active issuer-linked dining program and a direct benchmark for what a restaurant reservation partner can do for card spend.
  - Angle: Reservation-platform dining earn or credit for Wells Fargo cardholders, or a competing arrangement with another reservation platform.

- **GigSky**: Travel eSIM data provider behind a card-linked benefit (15 days, 5GB). ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/us-bank-altitude-connect-gigsky-benefit-may-end-11-30-26/))
  - Signal: US Bank confirmed its Altitude Connect GigSky eSIM benefit may end on November 30, 2026.
  - Why it fits: A benefit sunset leaves a gap for travel-data perks, and eSIM roaming is a practical, low-cost benefit for international travelers.
  - Angle: eSIM travel data allowance as a lower-cost international travel perk on a premium card.
