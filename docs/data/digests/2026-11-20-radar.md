A thin week for true partnership plays. Five items are worth a conversation: two airline-linked loyalty moves, one new foreign airline card entrant, and two fintech or portal offer models. Hotel openings, auction results and BNPL stories were skipped as context only.

- **Bilt (with JetBlue)**: Rent-focused rewards program that runs points promos and airline partner tie-ins. ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/bilt-rent-day-up-to-a-100-bonus-when-using-points-a-statement-credit-jetblue-mosaic-challenge/))
  - Signal: Rent Day offers up to a 100% bonus when points are redeemed for statement credits (normally 0.55 cents per point), plus a JetBlue Mosaic status match.
  - Why it fits: A status match is a low-cost, high-perceived-value perk that an airline gives away, and it shows how a card program can package partner status as an engagement driver.
  - Angle: A time-limited airline status match or challenge for premium cardholders, paired with a points redemption boost.

- **Etihad**: Abu Dhabi-based airline with its own loyalty program. ([Upgraded Points](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiZkFVX3lxTE1hSVo4ZlY4VkNsZzFPSWR3M00zOHpLZlppN1o4SHJod2dqODVGSE1PNWNlZy0wQ0pyN0VKZWYzdTQ4SkN2OU5EWW5tWGt1SXRXRVlkd3psYWhlMXJxd1laSGhVR1I4UQ?oc=5))
  - Signal: Reported as launching its first credit cards on the U.S. market. The source gives no issuer or benefit details, so those are unknown.
  - Why it fits: A foreign premium carrier building a U.S. card base may look for issuing, benefit or lounge partners, and it will compete for the same international travelers.
  - Angle: Find out who is issuing for Etihad and whether there is room for a transfer, lounge or international travel benefit tie-in.

- **Rove**: Loyalty program that earns miles on spending. ([The Points Guy](https://thepointsguy.com/news/rove-personalized-shopping-offers/))
  - Signal: Launched personalized shopping offers with up to 25 miles per dollar spent, and expanded in-store earning.
  - Why it fits: A program building a personalized merchant-offer layer suggests appetite for offer and redemption partners, and its card-linked mechanics are close to what we run.
  - Angle: Co-promoted merchant offers or a points conversion option for cardholders who shop in-store and online.

- **Revolut (Velocity Points)**: Global fintech and neobank. ([Executive Traveller](https://news.google.com/rss/articles/CBMiYEFVX3lxTE4zQWpQZTBhVGZtRnlMcndGZUFjX0ZPbGFlZWZCMG11NjluVzNvQ2ZLcXBycy1GWUp5MjZ1S2w2OUVDY19mSkhvUjlTZ2QyeERNRHVYSnJidjlaNkdFdXgtaA?oc=5))
  - Signal: Revolut has added Velocity Points as a points currency it supports.
  - Why it fits: A fintech plugging into an airline loyalty currency shows a model where a non-bank provides airline redemption value, and it competes for the same affluent travelers.
  - Angle: Take: assess how the Revolut and Velocity link works, then compare it with our own airline transfer and redemption options.

- **Rakuten (with Citi)**: Cashback and shopping portal. ([Frequent Miler](https://frequentmiler.com/rakuten-offering-cashback-on-citi-cards/))
  - Signal: Offering $50 cashback or up to 5,000 Bilt points as approval bonuses on three Citi cards. The source also names Chase and Bank of America.
  - Why it fits: A portal acting as an issuer acquisition channel with a choice of cash or points currency is a model that could be replicated or countered.
  - Angle: Take: explore a portal-funded approval bonus or points-currency choice for our cards, and watch how Citi's results develop.
